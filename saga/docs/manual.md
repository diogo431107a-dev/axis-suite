# The Habits Saga manual

The reference for the game. Part 1 is what you need to play well, above all how to classify your habits. Part 2 is how everything works underneath, and it spoils what the game means you to discover.

The game itself explains very little, on purpose. [The philosophy](philosophy.md) says why. The **?** at the top of every screen opens a short help.

## Part 1: Playing

### The idea

Your habits become companions and your one-off tasks become spells. When you do a habit in real life, its companion attacks. When you finish a task, a companion casts it. The road of foes only moves forward when you do.

Nothing checks that you really did it. The game runs on trust, and cheating costs you the only thing it offers.

### The eight attributes

Every habit trains one attribute, and this is the most important choice you make. It sets the companion's class and spell school, and it decides where the companion's points land on the hero's sheet.

**To decide, ask what the habit is for on the day you most want to skip it.** Stretching to keep your back from hurting is Vitality. Stretching to reach a new range of motion is Body. If two attributes still seem right, pick the one you neglect more.

**Resolve is the one exception.** The others ask what area of life a habit belongs to. Resolve asks how far past your limit it goes. See its entry.

#### 🏃 Body: things that move you

Class: Brawler. School: Force.

- **Belongs here:** Pilates, walking, running, lifting, dancing, cycling, taking the stairs on purpose, any exercise.
- **Not here:** sleep, meals, and rest days (Vitality).
- **Boundary:** gentle stretching to recover is Vitality. If the movement itself is the goal, it's Body.

#### 🔥 Vitality: things that maintain the body

Class: Herbalist. School: Mend.

- **Belongs here:** sleeping on time, cooking a real meal, drinking water, taking medication, a rest day taken on purpose, a dental routine, doctor's check-ups.
- **Not here:** training (Body), cleaning the kitchen (Order).
- **Boundary:** going to bed by eleven is Vitality. Getting up at five for the gym is Body, or Resolve if five is well past your limit.

#### 📚 Mind: things that add knowledge

Class: Scholar. School: Insight.

- **Belongs here:** reading, audiobooks, a course, language study, documentaries you actually attend to, working through a textbook.
- **Not here:** practising a skill by producing things (Craft), reading for spiritual practice (Spirit).
- **Boundary:** studying music theory is Mind. Playing scales on the guitar is Craft. If the result is something you know, it's Mind. If the result is something you can make or a thing that now exists, it's Craft.

#### 🎸 Craft: things you make

Class: Bard. School: Anthem.

- **Belongs here:** guitar, drawing, writing, composing, woodwork, code you write for its own sake, anything that leaves something behind or trains your hands to.
- **Not here:** routine paid work with no making in it (Order, if it keeps your affairs in hand).
- **Boundary:** cooking as daily nourishment is Vitality. Cooking as something you're learning to do beautifully is Craft.

#### 🕯️ Spirit: things that deepen your practice

Class: Mystic. School: Purge.

- **Belongs here:** meditation, prayer, ritual, contemplation, journaling to examine yourself, study of the texts of your practice.
- **Not here:** reading philosophy out of curiosity (Mind), a walk just for exercise (Body).
- **Boundary:** the practice is yours to define, whatever your tradition or lack of one. What marks it is that you come back changed, not better informed.

#### 🧹 Order: things that keep the house and your affairs in hand

Class: Warden. School: Ward.

- **Belongs here:** tidying, laundry, dishes, paying bills, reviewing your budget, inbox to zero, planning the week, renewing documents.
- **Not here:** people-related upkeep such as replying to a friend (Charisma).
- **Boundary:** a weekly review of your plans is Order. Forcing yourself through the paperwork you have dodged for six months is Resolve.

#### ⚓ Resolve: exceptional work and extra effort

Class: Knight. School: Finish.

Resolve is for what goes beyond what you normally do. It covers the extra hour, the unusual effort, and the hard thing done when easier ones were available. It used to be only for finished projects, and finishing a long project still counts, but you no longer need one.

- **Belongs here:** one more set or page past your stopping point, a deep-work block longer than usual, doing the thing you've been avoiding the longest, finishing a project.
- **Not here:** anything you already do comfortably. Once the extra hour has become your normal, it is no longer extra. Raise the bar, or bench the companion.
- **Tasks too:** a task in the Finish school is one that took unusual effort, however it's filed otherwise. Calling the plumber is an Order task. Finally having the call you've dreaded for a year is a Finish task.
- **Boundary:** the activity doesn't decide this, the effort does. Writing a page a day is Craft. Staying at the desk to write the page you didn't want to is Resolve.

#### 🎭 Charisma: likes and social things

Class: Envoy. School: Charm.

- **Belongs here:** calling a friend, replying to messages, showing up to gatherings, meeting someone new, sharing your work where people can respond to it, thanking someone properly.
- **Not here:** scrolling (that's more likely a vice), the making itself before you share it (Craft).
- **Boundary:** writing the song is Craft. Playing it for people and talking with them afterwards is Charisma.

### Camp

Between battles you are at camp. Here you see the hero's sheet, the foe that blocks the road, your party, the inn and the chronicle of what has happened.

The hero has no powers of their own. The sheet sums up your companions: every level a companion gains adds points to its attribute, and the total sets the hero's level.

### Companions and the party

Call a companion on the Roster, and your habit becomes one. Up to five fight in the party at once, and the rest wait on the bench. A new companion joins the party if there is room, and waits on the bench if not. Only the party can act, and only those who fight share in the rewards.

Each companion's card has two small buttons. **Note** keeps your own words on the habit: why you keep it, what counts as doing it, what the goal is now. **Skills** shows its mastery, spell and flee levels.

Choose your five according to what you are working on now, not what you wish you were. Swap them whenever your focus changes.

### A turn of battle

Each companion gets one action per turn:

- **Attack** when you've just done its habit.
- **Spell** when you've just finished one of its school's tasks. It costs MP and takes the task off the list.
- **Item** to use something from the bag on a party member.
- **Guard** to take half damage until the turn ends.

**Keys 1 to 5** attack with each party member. When nobody can act, or you press End turn, the foe takes its turn. Then the next turn begins.

Do the habit first, and then attack. If you did it twice, you still only get one attack per turn. End the turn and take the next one.

Once a battle starts, the only ways out are winning or running.

### Winning, falling and running

- **Victory** pays XP, gold and fame, and moves the road on to the next foe.
- **Knocked out:** a companion at 0 HP can't act and loses some XP. Only a Phoenix Feather brings them back.
- **The party falls:** when everyone is down, they all wake at camp with 1 HP. The foe stands whole again. You lose no gold.
- **Running** may fail. If it does, the party loses its turn and may drop some gold. A foe you escape keeps its wounds until you come back.

### Tasks and the Spellbook

Tasks are one-off things to do. Each belongs to a school, and only a companion of that school can cast it. A cast is how you record the task as done. It pays nothing directly, but the companion's spell level grows with every cast.

If you have a task and no companion of its school, you'll need to recruit one or wait. That limit is deliberate.

### Vices

A vice is a bad habit you name on the Roster. Each time it gets the better of you, **pay the tithe**, honestly, in or out of battle. Paying never uses a turn. It takes points from one attribute on the hero's sheet and costs gold. If you can't pay, an ailment strikes the party instead. The game always says what a tithe will cost before you confirm.

Points taken by a vice stay taken. Repeating a vice makes it cost more for a while, and victories cool it down.

You choose how much each vice costs when you create it. Be honest about that too.

### The shop and the inn

| Item                  | Price | Effect                                            |
| --------------------- | ----- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| Health Potion         | 30    | Restores 40% of max HP to a member still standing |
| Ether                 | 25    | Restores 15 MP to a member still standing         |
| Phoenix Feather       | 90    | Revives a knocked-out member with 1 HP            |
| Elite Phoenix Feather | 220   | Revives a knocked-out member with 25% of max HP   |
| Antidote              | 20    | Cures Poison                                      |
| Remedy                | 60    | Cures every ailment                               |

In battle, using an item takes a companion's action. At camp it's free.

The **inn** restores every standing companion's HP and MP and cures their ailments. It never revives anyone. Its price follows the region you're in, so travelling back to an easier region is a cheap way to recover.

### Travel

From camp you can travel back to any foe you have passed. It starts at full health, while the foe at the front keeps its wounds until you return. Defeating a foe you travelled to brings you back to the front.

### The road, fame and the Hall of Fame

Eight regions, six foes each, the last of each being a boss:

| Tier | Region          | Foes                    |
| ---- | --------------- | ----------------------- |
| I    | Cellar Meadows  | Crawlers                |
| II   | Greywood        | Beasts                  |
| III  | Twilight Grove  | Tricksters              |
| IV   | Hollow Crypts   | Restless Dead           |
| V    | Sunken Ruins    | Elementals & Constructs |
| VI   | Highland Pass   | Giants                  |
| VII  | Ember Cliffs    | Drakes                  |
| VIII | The Ember Peaks | Dragons                 |

After the Elder Dragon, the road starts again, one star stronger.

**Fame** comes from foes defeated, and bigger foes are worth far more. **Level** comes from the attribute sheet. The public Hall of Fame ranks heroes by either, and a hero needs at least one habit to be listed. Fame also earns the hero's title.

## Part 2: Behind the scenes

**Spoilers.** The game means you to discover this by playing. These are the numbers in version 0.0.12, and they will change.

### Levels

Every progression needs `base × level^1.8` to reach the next level.

| Progression     | Measured in      | Base | To reach level 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 |
| --------------- | ---------------- | ---- | ---------------------------- |
| Companion level | XP               | 20   | 20, 70, 144, 243, 362        |
| Mastery         | Attacks made     | 2    | 2, 7, 14, 24, 36             |
| Spell level     | Casts            | 3    | 3, 10, 22, 36, 54            |
| Flee skill      | Escapes led      | 4    | 4, 14, 29, 49, 72            |
| Hero level      | Attribute points | 10   | 10, 35, 72, 121, 181         |

A companion has `40 + 10 × level` max HP and `6 + 2 × level` max MP, and it carries 3 attribute points per level. A level-up fully restores HP and MP (if the companion is standing) and clears its knockout streak.

### Attacks

`damage = (8 + 4 × level + points) × 1.06^(mastery − 1)`

Points are the companion's own, 3 per level, so at mastery 1 a level 1 companion hits for 15 and a level 5 for 43. The result varies by ±10%. There is a 10% chance of a critical hit (×1.5). Shock halves the damage. Blind gives a 50% chance of missing, and the habit still counts. Nothing else causes a miss.

### Spells

A spell costs `3 + spell level` MP. Its strength grows with `f = 1 − 0.85^spell level`.

| School  | Effect                                                | Level 1 | Level 3 | Level 5 | Level 10 |
| ------- | ----------------------------------------------------- | ------- | ------- | ------- | -------- |
| Force   | Hits for `0.3 + 1.6f` of an attack                    | 54%     | 92%     | 119%    | 159%     |
| Mend    | Heals each standing member `0.4f` of max HP           | 6%      | 15%     | 22%     | 32%      |
| Insight | Foe's next turn does `0.7f` less damage               | 11%     | 27%     | 39%     | 56%      |
| Anthem  | Party attacks deal `+0.8f` for the turn               | +12%    | +31%    | +45%    | +64%     |
| Purge   | Cures, and heals `0.2f` of max HP                     | 3%      | 8%      | 11%     | 16%      |
| Ward    | Party takes `0.9f` less damage for the turn (max 75%) | 14%     | 35%     | 50%     | 72%      |
| Finish  | Hits for `0.2 + 1.2f` of an attack, up to ×3          | 38%     | 66%     | 87%     | 116%     |
| Charm   | `0.7f` chance the foe loses its next turn             | 11%     | 27%     | 39%     | 56%      |

Purge cures Poison and Burn at level 1, adds Shock and Blind at level 2, and cures everything from level 3. Finish's multiplier is `1 + 2 × the share of HP the foe has lost`.

### Sharing the spoils

XP is shared when a foe falls, and only among party members still standing and not cursed. 15% is split evenly. The other 85% follows **engagement**, a hidden score:

- Damage dealt, including extra damage from your Anthem on someone else's hit.
- Damage prevented by your Guard, Ward or Insight.
- Healing and curing, including with items.
- A charmed foe's lost turn.
- A bonus for the first hit (10% of the foe's max HP) and the last hit (15%).

Engagement against a foe carries over if you flee and come back, and it is lost when the whole party falls. Attacking and casting pay no XP in themselves. They train mastery and spell level.

A foe's move multiplies the damage unit by its size (small ×0.6, medium ×1, large ×1.6, heavy ×2.2) and varies by ±20%. Each star on the road adds 50% to a foe's HP, damage and XP. Each star also adds one more full share of gold and fame.

Rats bite. Beetles, slugs and toadstools swipe. Centipedes, bats and skeletons maul. Spiders and serpents bite and bite back. Beasts and chests savage. Imps bite or swipe. Ghosts and slimes sweep. Giants slam. Dragons breathe. Bosses and some dragons combine these.

### Knockouts

A knockout costs XP: 10% of the current level's requirement, plus 5% for each repeat, up to 30%. It never takes a companion below the start of their level. The repeat count clears at the next level-up.

### Fleeing

`chance = 85% − 10% per tier above I − 10% against a boss − 5% per star + 4% per flee level above 1`, kept between 5% and 95%. The party uses its best flee level among those standing. That companion leads the escape, and only a successful escape trains the skill.

A failed escape costs the party its turn. It may also drop gold, with a chance of `60% − 5% per flee level above 1` (at least 15%). The amount is 5–15% of the purse.

### Vices and ailments

A vice's cost and drain grow by ×1.25 per point of **heat**. Each tithe paid adds 1 heat, and each victory cools every vice by 1. New vices default to 2 points and 10 gold.

If the purse can't pay, one random ailment strikes one random standing member, or the whole party one time in three. **Ailments come only from unpaid vices.** Foes never inflict them.

| Ailment | Lasts       | Effect                                  |
| ------- | ----------- | --------------------------------------- |
| Poison  | Until cured | Loses 8% of max HP at the end of a turn |
| Burn    | 3 turns     | Loses 5% of max HP when acting          |
| Freeze  | 1 turn      | Can't act                               |
| Shock   | 2 turns     | Deals half damage                       |
| Blind   | 2 turns     | Attacks may miss                        |
| Curse   | 3 turns     | Gets no share of victory XP             |

### The inn and the start

The inn costs 15 gold × the tier of the region you're in. A new hero starts with 80 gold, 3 Health Potions, 1 Ether, 1 Phoenix Feather and 1 Antidote.
