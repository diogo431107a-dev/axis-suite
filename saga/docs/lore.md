# The lore of Habits Saga

**Draft for review.** Every name here can still change. Regions, foes and titles come from the game as it is. The rest is new.

This is what the game's labels will draw from. The player meets it in fragments and never as a lecture: a line from a foe, a word from the innkeeper, a title that changes.

## The Saga of the Turning

### Before the road

Heaven and Hell have been at war for longer than there have been mornings, and they have never agreed on anything but this: a soul must move to be worth having.

A soul that rises can be saved. A soul that falls can be claimed. Everything the two powers ever fought over was a person in motion, leaning one way or the other, and for an age that was all the world contained.

Then something woke beneath the Ember Peaks.

No one made the Elder Dragon. It is simply the oldest thing that ever did the same thing twice. It did so again, and again, until the repeating had grown scales. It wants nothing and hates no one. It breathes, and where its breath settles, people go on doing today what they did yesterday, and they call this peace.

Its children went down from the peaks, each smaller than the last. Drakes on the cliffs, giants in the pass, the dead in their crypts, tricksters in the grove, beasts in the wood. Lowest and last, the crawling things in the meadows, which hardly seem worth a boot. They do not kill anyone. A rat takes a crumb and a morning. A web goes up across a door that you meant to open later. You could live a long life under such small thefts, and most did.

And the souls stopped moving.

Heaven's halls emptied, because no one climbed. Hell's fires ran low, because no one fell. Those who stay exactly as they are can't be saved or damned, only kept, and the only one keeping them was the Dragon.

### The conspiracy

The story that the priests don't tell, and the devils tell only when drunk, is what happened next.

Two messengers met on a bare hill under a full moon, in the one place where the old treaty lets their kind stand together.

One was **Lunaria**, of the white wings and the golden ring. She had come to say that Heaven would rather lose a soul to Hell than see it lost to no one.

The other was **Alecta**, of the bat's wings and the dark blue mail, who sat on a dead tree above a sleeping city and had been waiting some time. She had come to say the same thing, with a better turn of phrase.

Neither of them may fight. That is the oldest law, and even the Dragon's rise did not break it: no angel or devil may strike a blow in the world of the living. They may only lean. They may whisper, and they may open a door or close one, and for a very short time they may stand beside someone who has already come a long way on their own.

So they made a wager, each sure of winning it. They would find one soul. Someone so far gone, so low, so thoroughly unpromising that the Dragon's children had stopped bothering with them. If that soul could turn, by no power but their own, then anyone could, and the whole world might start moving again. And then Heaven and Hell could go back to fighting over it properly.

They looked for a long time. In the end they found you.

### The hero

You woke in a cellar in the meadows. You owned little. The villagers had a name for you, and it was not kind. Not even your mother had a better one.

Nothing about that morning was different from the thousand before it. No light came down, and no voice spoke. You just got up and did one small thing that you had said you would do, and somewhere above the rooftops, two people who hate each other looked up.

A rat bit you. You hit it. That wasn't much of a blow, and it wasn't much of a rat.

But something strange happened. The next morning, when you did the thing again, someone was standing beside you who hadn't been there before.

### The companions

No one knows where they come from. The old songs say that they were always there, waiting in the shape of what you might do, and that each deed you repeat calls one of them out of the dark and gives them a little more of a body.

They are as loyal as your deeds and no more. A companion strikes only when you have truly done what they stand for. The Brawler comes when you move, the Herbalist when you tend yourself, the Scholar when you learn. The Bard answers what you make, the Mystic your practice, the Warden the order of your house. The Envoy goes out among people on your behalf.

And then there is the Knight, who comes least often and is hardest to keep. The Knight answers only when you go further than you meant to.

Only five can walk with you at one time. Nobody has ever said why. A hero who tries to lead more finds that they are leading none.

The spells they cast are the deeds done once and finished, each burned up in the casting.

### The road

There is one road, and it goes up.

**The Cellar Meadows** come first, where the crawlers live and you learn that you can win. **The Greywood** is next, and here the beasts run you down in the dark. In **the Twilight Grove** nothing is what it looks like, and the chest that opens its mouth was never a reward. **The Hollow Crypts** hold what is left of everything you ever started and dropped, and it knows your name. In **the Sunken Ruins**, old machinery still ticks, keeping time for no one. In **the Highland Pass** the giants charge a toll for the first step and don't care for your reasons. **The Ember Cliffs** are where the drakes strike from high ledges, and only those who have climbed high are worth striking.

Then **the Ember Peaks**, and the dragons, and the oldest one at the top. It remembers you. It has always been there. Its first words are said to be, "You again."

### What it costs

Everything you do on the road has a price, and the road keeps accounts.

**Vices** are tithes paid to the Dragon. They take strength from you, and it does not come back. Whatever you've given them, they will ask for more the next time. Their appetite only eases with victory. If you can't pay, the debt falls on those who walk with you, as sickness: poison, burn, frost, a blindness that passes. The Mystics can draw it out.

**The fallen** can't get up alone. Phoenix feathers are shed, it is said, by things that have died and thought better of it. They aren't cheap, and the inn won't help. An innkeeper can do many things, but raising the dead has never been on the bill.

**Defeat** is not the end. That is the strangest part, and the part both Lunaria and Alecta were counting on. When your whole party falls and the foe stands over you, you wake at camp, with almost nothing left, and everything you had won against that foe is gone. The foe is whole again. It is still there, and so are you.

No one on this road has ever died of losing. People only stop. It looks the same from a distance, but it's a very different thing.

### The Angel's place

Lunaria and Alecta keep watching. They are not your friends.

Lunaria will tell you that you're worth saving, and she means it, and she hopes you'll be grateful. Alecta will tell you that you're worth having, and she means that too, and she has noticed things about you that Lunaria has been too polite to mention.

Beside every hero there is one place that no companion can fill. It is called the Angel's place, and by every right it is Lunaria's. But the law gives the hero the choosing of who stands there, and the law, as Alecta likes to point out, says nothing about wings.

Choose Lunaria, and the rightful Angel takes her place. Choose Alecta, and she sits down in it as if it had been built for her. She answers to Angel, wears the title with no right to it at all, and loves every moment. Lunaria has not forgiven this, and Alecta would be disappointed if she did.

It is said that a hero who has walked long enough, and struck often enough, will find the Angel's place filled. The law still holds: whoever stands there will not strike a blow. But for a short time she may stand with you, and those are the times the songs remember. Then the law drags her back, and you must walk that whole long way again before she can return.

Lunaria gives strength and shelter. Alecta gives her attention to your enemy, which your enemy will not enjoy. Both will claim the credit either way.

### The Hero of a Thousand Mornings

Some say that someone has reached the top of the road, and that the Elder Dragon fell.

They also say that the next morning they woke at the bottom of the road again, in the meadows, and that the rats were bigger.

That is the last thing both messengers know and neither one will tell you. The Dragon is the oldest habit in the world, and the only thing that can beat it is a newer one, kept up. There is no last battle and no morning when it's finished, and you are not going to be finished either. The wager was never about whether you would arrive. It was whether a person, from the very bottom, against all sense, could turn.

Now you know that they can, because you have.

In the Hall of Fame the heralds call that person the Hero of a Thousand Mornings, and they are still counting.

## For the label pass

The notes below are for us, not for the player. Phase 4 of the [roadmap](roadmap.md) uses them.

### The voice

- Earnest and mythic, with dry humour. The story takes itself seriously. The people in it sometimes don't.
- The world never mentions the real world. No "notifications", "tabs" or "snooze button". The real-life meaning is there for those who see it.
- The villagers supply the mockery, and it fades as you climb. "Not Even Your Mother Likes You" is what they call you in the first week. "Hero of a Thousand Mornings" is what they call you later, more quietly.
- Say less than you know.
